\section{Galois embedding registers over an arbitrary field}
\label{sec:galois-embedding-registers}

A finite separable extension carries a finite set of embeddings into a
separable closure, even when the ground field is infinite. This set gives
a finite quantum register with a natural Galois action. Its dimension is
the extension degree. Over a finite field it realizes a single primitive
Frobenius orbit. The construction below includes every finite separable
field tower and the finite \'etale algebras required by independent tensor.
The three results have passed the capped independent review.

\begin{definition}[Finite \'etale embedding datum]
Fix a field $K$ and a named separable closure $\Omega=K^{\mathrm{sep}}$.
Put $G_K=\operatorname{Aut}_K(\Omega)$ with its Krull topology. Let
$\mathrm{Et}_K^+$ have as objects nonzero finite \'etale commutative unital
$K$-algebras and as arrows unital $K$-algebra homomorphisms $f:A\to B$
for which $B$ is finite locally free over $A$ of one constant positive
rank $r_f$. Equivalently, $B$ is isomorphic to $A^{r_f}$ as an $A$-module;
no such module isomorphism is chosen. The tensor is $\otimes_K$ and its
unit is $K$. Set
\[
 X_A=\operatorname{Hom}_{K\text{-alg}}(A,\Omega),\qquad
 n_A=\dim_K A,\qquad H_A^{\mathrm{emb}}=\ell^2(X_A),
\]
\[
 \mathcal B_A^{\mathrm{emb}}=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}
       (H_A^{\mathrm{emb}}),\qquad
 U_A^{\mathrm{emb}}(g)|\tau\rangle=|g\circ\tau\rangle,\qquad
 \rho_A^{\mathrm{mix}}=I/n_A.
\]
The basis of $\ell^2(X_A)$ is orthonormal for counting measure. The
observable algebra is the full matrix algebra, including coherent matrix
entries between distinct $G_K$-orbits. The reference trace is ordinary
matrix trace, and the named reference density is $\rho_A^{\mathrm{mix}}$.
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
Constant rank is a condition on every component of $A$. It includes every
finite separable field extension, with no restriction on characteristic or
on whether $K$ is perfect. The superscript distinguishes this register
from the register with all field elements as basis labels.
\end{scope}

\begin{definition}[Embedding-fibre transfers and comparison maps]
For a finite \'etale embedding datum and $f:A\to B$, define
\[
 R_f:X_B\longrightarrow X_A,\quad R_f(\tau)=\tau\circ f,
 \qquad
 s_f|\sigma\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_f}}
       \sum_{R_f(\tau)=\sigma}|\tau\rangle.
\]
The square root is the positive real square root. For objects $A,C$, let
\[
 \mu_{A,C}:H_A^{\mathrm{emb}}\otimes H_C^{\mathrm{emb}}
       \longrightarrow H_{A\otimes_K C}^{\mathrm{emb}}
\]
send $|\sigma\rangle\otimes|\tau\rangle$ to the basis vector of the
homomorphism $a\otimes c\mapsto\sigma(a)\tau(c)$. The unit comparison
sends $1\in\mathbb C$ to the unique $K$-embedding of $K$ into $\Omega$.
For a named $K$-isomorphism of separable closures
$\eta:\Omega\to\Omega'$, define
\[
 W_{\eta,A}|\tau\rangle=|\eta\circ\tau\rangle,
 \qquad c_\eta(g)=\eta g\eta^{-1}.
\]
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
No basis of $A$ over $K$ is chosen. A separable closure and any comparison
between two such closures are named data. A single normalization for
nonuniform embedding fibres is outside this definition.
\end{scope}

\begin{theorem}[Coherent quantization of finite \'etale towers]
\statusProved\quad
For every field $K$ and named separable closure $\Omega$, the category
$\mathrm{Et}_K^+$ is symmetric monoidal. Each restriction map $R_f$ is
surjective with every fibre of cardinality $r_f$. The assignments
$A\mapsto(H_A^{\mathrm{emb}},U_A^{\mathrm{emb}})$ and $f\mapsto s_f$,
with the displayed tensor and unit comparisons, give a faithful strong
symmetric monoidal functor to finite continuous unitary
$G_K$-representations and equivariant isometries. In particular,
\[
 s_f^*s_f=I,\qquad s_{h\circ f}=s_hs_f,\qquad
 U_B^{\mathrm{emb}}(g)s_f=s_fU_A^{\mathrm{emb}}(g),
\]
\[
 s_{f\otimes k}\mu_{A,C}=\mu_{B,D}(s_f\otimes s_k)
       \quad(f:A\to B,\ k:C\to D).
\]
Every named change $\eta$ of separable closure gives a natural monoidal
unitary comparison, transports the Galois action through $c_\eta$, and
satisfies $W_{\theta\eta,A}=W_{\theta,A}W_{\eta,A}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{scope}
The result includes all finite separable field towers over every $K$ and
all allowed \'etale towers. Faithfulness concerns the specified algebra
maps; it does not assert fullness among equivariant isometries. A different
closure comparison $\eta'$ changes $W_\eta$ by
$U^{\mathrm{emb}}(\eta'\eta^{-1})$ on the target.
\end{scope}

\begin{proof}
Splitting over $\Omega$ identifies $A\otimes_K\Omega$ with
$\prod_{\sigma\in X_A}\Omega$. The coordinate idempotent at $\sigma$
cuts $B\otimes_K\Omega$ down to one coordinate for each extension of
$\sigma$ to $B$. Constant rank makes this dimension $r_f$.
Consequently the normalized columns of $s_f$ have disjoint support and
unit norm. A label in the top field of a tower has one uniquely determined
intermediate restriction, so the composite sum has coefficient
$(r_fr_h)^{-1/2}$ exactly once on every surviving label. This proves tower
composition.

An embedding of $A\otimes_K C$ is uniquely the product of one embedding
of each factor. All tensor comparisons therefore act on the same ordered
tuples. Reassociation and swap commute on these tuples, and fibre sizes
multiply. Galois postcomposition and closure changes biject every
restriction fibre and preserve its normalization. Finally, the support
of $s_f$ recovers $R_f$, and the embedding-set equivalence recovers $f$.
The source input is Milne, \emph{Fields and Galois Theory}, version 5.10,
Propositions 8.6, 8.9 and 8.20, Corollary 8.7 and Theorems 6.10 and 8.21;
the same equivalence is Stacks Lemma 58.2.2.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Retained embedding decoding]
For a finite \'etale embedding datum and its embedding-fibre transfers,
put $P_f=s_fs_f^*$ and $Q_f=I-P_f$, and use the retained-isometry decoder
schema in the full complex matrix algebras. Its encoding, success map
and retained decoder are
\[
 \mathcal E_f(\rho)=s_f\rho s_f^*,\qquad
 d_f(\rho)=s_f^*\rho s_f,
\]
\[
 \mathcal D_f(\rho)=\bigl(d_f(\rho),Q_f\rho Q_f\bigr):
 \mathcal B_B^{\mathrm{emb}}\longrightarrow
 \mathcal B_A^{\mathrm{emb}}\oplus\mathcal B_B^{\mathrm{emb}}.
\]
The output tags are success:$A$ and failure:$B$; trace on a tagged algebra
is the sum of ordinary matrix traces. A completely positive map is a
linear map whose identity-matrix amplifications preserve positive matrices;
it is trace preserving if it preserves this specified trace. Independent
decoders pair every output tag. A stopped tower decoder applies the next
decoder only on the preceding success output and retains every failure
at the object where it first occurs. Full instruments are compared with
these histories, without identifying them with the binary decoder of a
composite isometry.
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
Failure retains its quantum output. This is an instantiation of the
retained decoder for an isometry, with ordinary trace on each tag.
\end{scope}

\begin{theorem}[Positive decoding with all independent outcomes]
\statusProved\quad
For every arrow $f$ of a finite \'etale embedding datum, $\mathcal E_f$
and $\mathcal D_f$ are $G_K$-equivariant completely positive
trace-preserving maps, and $d_f$ is completely positive and trace
nonincreasing. They satisfy
\[
 \mathcal D_f(\mathcal E_f(\rho))=(\rho,0),\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}d_f(\rho_B^{\mathrm{mix}})=\frac1{r_f},\qquad
 d_f d_h=d_{h\circ f}.
\]
Conditioned reference success gives $\rho_A^{\mathrm{mix}}$.
Stopped tower instruments and independent products of decoders are
completely positive and trace preserving with every indicated history or
paired output. For two independent maximally mixed inputs their four
probabilities are
\[
 (r_f^{-1},1-r_f^{-1})\otimes(r_k^{-1},1-r_k^{-1}).
\]
Closure changes and Cartesian tensor comparisons transport all these branches.
\end{theorem}
\begin{scope}
Complete positivity holds on entangled inputs as well. Only the stated
independent reference inputs force product probabilities. Full instruments
retain their histories; no equality with the binary decoder of a composite
or tensor isometry is asserted.
\end{scope}

\begin{proof}
The decoder has amplitudes $s_f^*$ and $Q_f$, whose squared sum is
$P_f+Q_f^2=I$. Their matrix amplifications are positive by the same
conjugation formula, and the squared-sum identity gives trace preservation.
Two independent decoders have all four tensor products of these amplitudes,
with squared sum $(P_f+Q_f)\otimes(P_k+Q_k)=I$.
In a tower $A\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{h}C$, the stopped history has
amplitudes $s_f^*s_h^*$, $Q_fs_h^*$ and $Q_h$, with output registers
$A,B,C$ respectively. Their squared sum is
$s_h(P_f+Q_f)s_h^*+Q_h=I$. Replacing any success by one more complete
decoder preserves this identity, proving arbitrary finite towers.

The difference between histories and a binary decoder is measurable.
Write $R=s_hQ_fs_h^*$, so $Q_{h\circ f}=R+Q_h$ with orthogonal summands.
Re-encoding and merging the two failure histories gives
$R\rho R+Q_h\rho Q_h$; the binary decoder gives
$(R+Q_h)\rho(R+Q_h)$. When both summands are nonzero, a superposition
between their ranges distinguishes these outputs by its off-diagonal terms.
Retaining the actual history is therefore part of the process specification.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Embedding stabilizer and normal closure]
For a finite \'etale embedding datum with $A$ a field and a named
$\tau_0\in X_A$, put
\[
 H_{A,\tau_0}=\{g\in G_K:g\circ\tau_0=\tau_0\},\qquad
 C_A=\bigcap_{g\in G_K}gH_{A,\tau_0}g^{-1},
\]
\[
 N_A=K\left(\bigcup_{\tau\in X_A}\tau(A)\right)\subset\Omega.
\]
Thus $N_A$ is the compositum of all embedded conjugates. Its definition
does not use $\tau_0$. The notation $C_A$ anticipates the proved independence
of its defining expression from the choice of $\tau_0$.
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
The extension $A/K$ is finite separable and need not be normal. The
stabilizer itself depends on the chosen embedding.
\end{scope}

\begin{definition}[Primitive finite-field orbit comparison]
For a finite \'etale embedding datum with $K=\mathbb F_p$, let
$\operatorname{Frob}_p:\Omega\to\Omega$ be $x\mapsto x^p$. Let $A$
be a degree-$d$ field extension of $\mathbb F_p$, choose $a\in A$ with
$A=\mathbb F_p(a)$, and choose $\tau_0\in X_A$. Put $b=\tau_0(a)$ and
$O_b=\{b,b^p,\ldots,b^{p^{d-1}}\}$. For a positive multiple $r$ of $d$,
let $E=\mathbb F_{p^r}$ be the unique such subfield of $\Omega$ and define
\[
 T_a:H_A^{\mathrm{emb}}\longrightarrow\ell^2(O_b)\subset\ell^2(E),
 \qquad T_a|\tau\rangle=|\tau(a)\rangle.
\]
The orbit coordinate unitary $L_{\tau_0}:\mathbb C^d\to H_A^{\mathrm{emb}}$
sends $|j\rangle$ to $|\operatorname{Frob}_p^j\circ\tau_0\rangle$ for
$0\leq j<d$. In these coordinates write $S_d|j\rangle=|j+1\bmod d\rangle$.
The word primitive here means degree exactly $d$ over $\mathbb F_p$, and
does not require a generator of $A$'s multiplicative group.
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
The comparison names a primitive element and cyclic origin. It compares
one orbit subspace; the full field-label register has dimension $p^r$.
\end{scope}

\begin{theorem}[Normal-closure symmetry and primitive Frobenius atoms]
\statusProved\quad
For every finite separable field extension $A/K$ and named separable
closure, $N_A/K$ is the finite Galois normal closure and
\[
 \ker U_A^{\mathrm{emb}}=C_A,\qquad
 \operatorname{Im}U_A^{\mathrm{emb}}\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(N_A/K).
\]
The isomorphism sends $U_A^{\mathrm{emb}}(g)$ to $g|_{N_A}$. It is
independent of the choice of $\tau_0$. Every finite separable tower
$A\to B$ gives $N_A\subset N_B$ and the corresponding restriction
quotient of these images. Over all finite Galois subfields $E$ of $\Omega$,
\[
 G_K\simeq\varprojlim_E\operatorname{Im}U_E^{\mathrm{emb}}
\]
as profinite groups, with the stated restriction transitions.
For $K=\mathbb F_p$, a degree-$d$ extension and the primitive orbit
comparison above satisfy
\[
 L_{\tau_0}^*U_A^{\mathrm{emb}}(\operatorname{Frob}_p)L_{\tau_0}=S_d.
\]
The map $T_a$ is a Frobenius-equivariant unitary onto the primitive
length-$d$ orbit subspace. Its full observable algebra and maximally mixed
state identify with the cycle block $M_d(\mathbb C)$ and $I/d$.
For $d>1$ this is a nonfixed-label orbit block.
\end{theorem}
\begin{scope}
Nonabelian images are included. The inverse limit retains the finite
Galois actions and their restriction maps; bare matrix algebras alone do
not reconstruct them. The finite-field comparison specifies the orbit
block and normalized block trace, with no assertion about orbit
multiplicities, boundary mixture weights, field-label code transfers,
Fourier transforms or multiplication processes.
\end{scope}

\begin{proof}
Every embedding extends to a Galois automorphism of the separable closure,
so $X_A\simeq G_K/H_{A,\tau_0}$. An element fixes every coset precisely
when it lies in every conjugate stabilizer. Fixing those embeddings is
equivalent to fixing their image compositum pointwise. The Galois
correspondence therefore identifies this kernel with
$\operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/N_A)$ and its quotient with
$\operatorname{Gal}(N_A/K)$. This is the normal-core description of the
normal closure in Milne, Remark 3.18. Stacks Lemmas 9.22.2--9.22.3 and
Theorem 9.22.4 give the restriction quotients and the profinite inverse limit.

Over $\mathbb F_p$, the embeddings are
$\tau_j=\operatorname{Frob}_p^j\tau_0$, $0\leq j<d$, by Milne,
Propositions 4.19--4.23. Evaluation at a primitive element makes their
images distinct, so it identifies their basis with one length-$d$ orbit.
Changing $\tau_0$ shifts the cyclic coordinates; changing the primitive
element gives the explicit comparison $T_{a'}T_a^*$ between the named
orbit spaces. Both comparisons intertwine Frobenius.
\end{proof}

For standard subfields $\mathbb F_{p^d}\subset\mathbb F_{p^e}$ with
$d\mid e$, the cyclic coordinates give
\[
 s_f|j\rangle=\sqrt{\frac de}
       \sum_{k=0}^{e/d-1}|j+kd\rangle.
\]
Thus this transfer pulls back a quotient of cycles. For example its
dimensions for $\mathbb F_2\subset\mathbb F_4$ are $1\to2$, whereas
the field-label inclusion has dimensions $2\to4$. The independent tensor
of degree-$d$ and degree-$e$ atoms has $\gcd(d,e)$ simultaneous-Frobenius
orbits of length $\operatorname{lcm}(d,e)$, but retains the full
$M_{de}(\mathbb C)$, including coherence between different orbits.

Separability is essential to the stated formula. If a finite extension
$L/K$ is not separable, a $K$-embedding into $K^{\mathrm{sep}}$ would make
every element of $L$ separable, a contradiction. Its embedding set here
is empty. Using an algebraic closure instead counts separable degree and
still loses inseparable multiplicity. The positive construction above
therefore supplies the Galois part of the arithmetic problem uniformly
over all base fields, while additional arithmetic data remain necessary
for a construction that also retains Fourier and multiplication diagrams.
