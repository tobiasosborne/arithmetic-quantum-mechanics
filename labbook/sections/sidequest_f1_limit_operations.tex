\section{Typed operations, classical histories and collective context}
\label{sec:f1-limit-operations}

The amplitude category has morphisms only within a fixed total degree.  An
operational theory needs more: preparation, discard, measurement and channels
must be arrows between observable algebras.  We build those arrows in
Heisenberg orientation, retain their Kraus data, and keep two kinds of
composition separate.  Juxtaposing registers produces a spatial tensor
algebra; assembling them produces one larger collective corner whose
observable algebra usually contains more operators.

\subsection{Registers and primitive processes}

\begin{definition}[Quantum atoms, classical wires and register words]
For every nonempty composition $\alpha$, let $[\alpha]$ be a quantum atom
with observable algebra
\[
 A_\alpha(q)=e_\alpha(q)H_{|\alpha|}(q)e_\alpha(q),
 \qquad \tau_{\alpha,q}=P_\alpha(q)\tau_{|\alpha|,q}.
\]
A register word is a finite ordered word of quantum atoms and classical
wires.  The algebra and reference trace of the word are the ordered spatial
tensor products of the factor algebras and traces; the empty word has algebra
$\mathbb C$.  For a nonempty finite set $O$, the wire $\underline O$ has
algebra $\mathbb C^O$ and uniform trace
\[
 \tau_O(f)=|O|^{-1}\sum_{o\in O}f(o).
\]
The separated word $[\alpha][\beta]$ and collective atom
$[\alpha\mathbin\Vert\beta]$ are different objects.
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
The empty outcome set is excluded from deterministic instruments.  A
classical wire has a commutative algebra; a coherent additive direct sum in
the completed amplitude category instead has matrix off-diagonal observables.
\end{scope}

\begin{definition}[Finite Heisenberg UCP category]
Objects are finite-dimensional unital C*-algebras.  An arrow $A\to B$ is a
unital completely positive map $\Phi:B\to A$.  If $A\to B\to C$ are
represented by $\Phi:B\to A$ and $\Psi:C\to B$, their composite is
$\Phi\circ\Psi:C\to A$; tensor is the spatial tensor of maps.  For faithful
reference traces, the density dual is the unique map satisfying
\[
 \tau_B\bigl(\Phi_*(\rho)a\bigr)
   =\tau_A\bigl(\rho\Phi(a)\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
This target records operational UCP maps and their density duals.  It does not
assert that every such map possesses a preferred Hecke-corner Kraus label.
\end{scope}

\begin{definition}[Preparations, measurements and retained instruments]
For a register word $X$, a preparation is a density
$h\geq0$, $\tau_X(h)=1$, with Heisenberg functional
$a\mapsto\tau_X(ha)$.  Discard is $\lambda\mapsto\lambda1_X$.  A POVM with
outcome set $O$ is a family $e_o\geq0$, $\sum_oe_o=1_X$, and realizes
\[
 \mathbb C^O\longrightarrow A_X,\qquad
 f\longmapsto\sum_of(o)e_o.
\]
An effect $0\leq e\leq1$ is the two-outcome POVM $(e,1-e)$.

For equal-total compositions $\alpha,\,\beta$, a corner instrument is a
finite family
\[
 K_{o,i}\in e_\beta H_n(q)e_\alpha,\qquad
 \sum_{o,i}K_{o,i}^*K_{o,i}=e_\alpha,
\]
with type
\[
 K:[\alpha]\longrightarrow[\beta]\underline O
\]
and Heisenberg realization
\[
 \Phi_K((a_o)_o)=\sum_{o,i}K_{o,i}^*a_oK_{o,i}.
\]
At one fibre, two labels are equal when the normalized-basis coefficient
Gram matrices agree separately in each outcome.  Equivalently, after zero
padding they differ by a scalar-unitary mixing of the Kraus index within each
outcome.
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
The quantum output of an instrument is retained.  A POVM alone specifies
effects but no postmeasurement state.  Kraus indices may be mixed within an
outcome, never across different classical outcomes.
\end{scope}
\provenance{D1207}{theory/sidequests/f1-limit/operational-category.md}{theory/checks/f1\_operational\_check.py (H8); theory/checks/f1\_limit\_wiring\_check.py (W3,W4)}{theory/verdicts/f1-limit-adjudication.md}

The branch density for outcome $o$ is not the raw matrix sum when source and
target corner traces have different normalizations.  It is
\[
 T_o(\rho)=\frac{P_\alpha(q)}{P_\beta(q)}
             \sum_iK_{o,i}\rho K_{o,i}^*.
\]
Relative to $\tau_\beta\otimes\tau_O$, the $o$-block of the full recorded
density is $|O|T_o(\rho)$.  Cyclicity proves that its trace is precisely the
branch Born weight and that the sum of all branch masses is one.

\begin{proposition}[UCP realization of primitive operations]
{\normalfont\statusProved}\quad
For every $q>0$, every preparation, discard, finite POVM and normalized
corner instrument just defined is a typed UCP process.  Its Schrödinger dual
is positive and trace preserving, and the branch density has the displayed
corner and classical cardinality factors.
\end{proposition}
\begin{scope}
This is a sufficient retained process class.  It makes no completeness claim
for arbitrary CP maps between two corner algebras.
\end{scope}
\provenance{F1-LIM-OP}{theory/sidequests/f1-limit/operational-category.md}{theory/checks/f1\_operational\_check.py (H8); theory/checks/f1\_limit\_wiring\_check.py (W3)}{theory/verdicts/f1-limit-adjudication.md}
\begin{proof}
Preparations are positive normalized functionals and discards are unital
*-maps.  At matrix level a positive element of
$M_r(\mathbb C^O)$ is a family of positive scalar matrices, so tensoring each
with $e_o$ and summing proves complete positivity of the POVM map.  Each
instrument summand has the form $a\mapsto K^*aK$; completeness makes their
sum unital.  The density formula follows from cyclicity:
\[
 \tau_\beta(T_o(\rho)a)
 =\tau_\alpha\!\left(\rho\sum_iK_{o,i}^*aK_{o,i}\right).
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Assembly is a retraction, not an identification}

\begin{definition}[Assembly and split]
For parabolic atoms, let
\[
 \operatorname{asm}_{\alpha,\beta}:
 [\alpha][\beta]\longrightarrow[\alpha\mathbin\Vert\beta]
\]
be represented in Heisenberg orientation by the trace-preserving conditional
expectation
\[
 E_{\alpha,\beta}:A_{\alpha\mathbin\Vert\beta}
       \longrightarrow A_\alpha\otimes A_\beta.
\]
Let $\operatorname{spl}_{\alpha,\beta}$ have the reverse type and be
represented by the corner inclusion
$j_{\alpha,\beta}:A_\alpha\otimes A_\beta
\to A_{\alpha\mathbin\Vert\beta}$.  The defining relations include the
three-block tower relations and
\[
 \operatorname{spl}\circ\operatorname{asm}
    =\operatorname{id}_{[\alpha][\beta]}.
\]
The reverse composite is the collective coarse graining $jE$.
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
The map $jE$ is generally proper and is not set equal to the collective
identity.  No assembly retraction is promoted to an isomorphism.
\end{scope}
\provenance{D1208}{theory/sidequests/f1-limit/operational-category.md}{theory/checks/f1\_limit\_check.py (L14); theory/checks/f1\_operational\_check.py (H3,H13)}{theory/verdicts/f1-limit-adjudication.md}

\begin{theorem}[Coherent collective assembly]
{\normalfont\statusProved}\quad
Assembly and split are associative UCP processes, $Ej=\mathrm{id}$, and
$jE$ is the conditional expectation onto the separated product subalgebra.
Preparing densities $h,k$ on separated registers and then assembling gives
the collective density $j(h\otimes k)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{scope}
The conclusion uses contiguous ordered blocks and the normalized corner
traces.  It supplies neither a block exchange nor equality of separated and
collective observables.
\end{scope}
\provenance{F1-LIM-ASM}{theory/sidequests/f1-limit/operational-category.md}{L14; H3,H13}{theory/verdicts/f1-limit-adjudication.md}
\begin{proof}
The Hecke block inclusion is a trace-preserving *-monomorphism and the
coefficient expectation fixes its range, giving $Ej=\mathrm{id}$.  The two
three-block inclusions and expectations agree on the standard basis, so the
processes are associative.  Trace duality gives
$\tau(j(h\otimes k)a)=(\tau_\alpha\otimes\tau_\beta)
((h\otimes k)E(a))$, proving the density statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Retained collective context and parallel lists]
For $K:[\alpha]\to[\beta]\underline O$ and a right context $\gamma$, retain
\[
 K\triangleleft\gamma:
 [\alpha\mathbin\Vert\gamma]
 \longrightarrow[\beta\mathbin\Vert\gamma]\underline O,
 \qquad
 (K\triangleleft\gamma)_{o,i}
   =\iota(K_{o,i}\otimes e_\gamma).
\]
For a second list $L:[\alpha']\to[\beta']\underline P$, the direct ordered
collective parallel instrument has type
\[
 K\boxtimes_{\mathrm c}L:
 [\alpha\mathbin\Vert\alpha']
 \longrightarrow[\beta\mathbin\Vert\beta']\underline{O\times P}
\]
and list $(\iota(K_{o,i}\otimes L_{p,j}))_{(o,p),(i,j)}$.
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
These lists act on full collective corner algebras.  They are not inferred
from the isolated CP shadows and preserve the displayed left-to-right order.
\end{scope}

\subsection{Classical history wires}

\begin{definition}[Classical products, relabeling and routing]
Fix a singleton $*$.  A bijection $r:O\to P$ gives
$\operatorname{rel}_r:\underline O\to\underline P$, represented by
$r^*:C^P\to C^O$, $r^*f=f\circ r$.  Classical product and unit are the
*-isomorphisms
\[
 \operatorname{mul}_{O,P}:\underline O\,\underline P
      \longrightarrow\underline{O\times P},
 \qquad \operatorname{unit}_{\mathrm c}:\underline *\longrightarrow\mathbf1,
\]
induced by $\delta_{(o,p)}\mapsto\delta_o\otimes\delta_p$ and
$\mathbb C^{\{*\}}\cong\mathbb C$; their inverses are included.  Cartesian associators and
singleton unitors are the corresponding relabelings.

For every object word $X$, the routing isomorphism
\[
 \operatorname{route}_{O,X}:\underline O,X\longrightarrow X\,\underline O
\]
is represented by the spatial flip
$A_X\otimes C^O\to C^O\otimes A_X$.  It is natural, involutive after
exchanging source and target, and satisfies the usual nesting and unit
relations.

For instruments $K:[\alpha]\to[\beta]\underline O$ and
$L:[\beta]\to[\gamma]\underline P$, define
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Seq}(L,K)
 &= (\operatorname{id}\otimes\operatorname{mul}_{O,P})
    (\operatorname{id}\otimes\operatorname{route}_{P,\underline O})
    (L\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\underline O})K,\\
 \operatorname{Seq}(L,K)&:[\alpha]\to[\gamma]\underline{O\times P}.
\end{align*}
Its branch list is $(L_{p,j}K_{o,i})_{(o,p),(j,i)}$.  For
$K':[\alpha']\to[\beta']\underline{P'}$, define
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Par}(K,K')
 &=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\operatorname{mul}_{O,P'})
   (\operatorname{id}_{[\beta]}\otimes
      \operatorname{route}_{O,[\beta']}\otimes
      \operatorname{id}_{\underline{P'}})(K\otimes K'),\\
 \operatorname{Par}(K,K')&:[\alpha][\alpha']
       \to[\beta][\beta']\underline{O\times P'}.
\end{align*}
Sequential associativity uses $((o,p),r)\leftrightarrow(o,(p,r))$;
sequential--parallel interchange uses
\[
 ((o,p),(o',p'))\longleftrightarrow((o,o'),(p,p')).
\]
One-outcome instruments use the singleton unitor. The single Kraus list
$(e_\alpha)$, followed by that unitor, is the identity process on $[\alpha]$;
its Heisenberg map is $a\mapsto e_\alpha a e_\alpha=a$.
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
Only a classical wire moves through a quantum output.  These maps do not
provide a quantum block commutor at generic $q$.
\end{scope}

\begin{definition}[Presented operational categories and evaluation]
At a fibre, take the strict ordered monoidal category generated by the
register objects, preparations, discards, POVMs, retained instruments,
assembly/split, context/parallel lists and classical wiring above.  Equality
is the congruence generated by typed planar isomorphism, category and monoidal
axioms, literal state and POVM equality, outcome-wise coefficient-Gram
equality, the routed history relations, context identities and assembly
tower relations.  It is not equality of one isolated CP shadow.

On an interval, labels are continuous and two Kraus labels are equal when
their coefficient Grams agree at every parameter.  At the endpoint, circuit
germs are equal when the presentation relation, and in particular Gram
equality, holds on a common smaller interval.  No continuous choice of a
mixing unitary is required.  Realization sends objects to their algebras and
generators to the stated UCP maps.  Evaluation substitutes the parameter in
every label; only endpoint evaluation is canonical for an arbitrary germ.
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
The realization may be nonfaithful.  Local lifting of one circuit will not
imply that every extra equation true only at the endpoint also lifts.
\end{scope}

\begin{theorem}[Sound typed operational category]
{\normalfont\statusProved}\quad
For every positive fibre, compact positive interval and endpoint germ, the
preceding generators and relations define an ordered monoidal category with
a monoidal UCP realization.  Routed sequential and parallel instruments are
normalized, history associativity and interchange are typed by the displayed
bijections, and pointwise or eventual Gram equality is a congruence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{scope}
No classical retyping is hidden in planar isotopy.  No quantum exchange is
asserted away from one, and no arbitrary CP-map completeness is claimed.
\end{scope}
\provenance{F1-LIM-OP}{theory/sidequests/f1-limit/operational-category.md; theory/sidequests/f1-limit/classical-wiring.md}{theory/checks/f1\_limit\_wiring\_check.py (W1--W5)}{theory/verdicts/f1-limit-adjudication.md}
\begin{proof}
Every wiring box is a trace-preserving *-isomorphism represented by a finite
permutation matrix.  Sequential completeness follows from
\[
 \sum_{o,p,i,j}K_{o,i}^*L_{p,j}^*L_{p,j}K_{o,i}=1,
\]
and parallel completeness factors into the two original sums.  The two
associative histories have the same branch operator and differ only by their
Cartesian bracketing.  Interchange follows from multiplicativity of the
ordered block inclusion and the stated history bijection.  At each fibre,
equal coefficient Grams are equivalent to outcome-wise scalar-unitary
mixing; tensoring those finite unitaries proves congruence pointwise, without
choosing them continuously.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A finite collective context detects retained Kraus data}

\begin{definition}[Local contextual recovery map]
For the complete $n$-constituent object, expand a retained list in the
normalized basis and put
\[
 J_K(k,h)=\sum_i a_i(k)\overline{a_i(h)}.
\]
Embed the local regular operator explicitly into rank $2n-1$ by
\[
 \widetilde B_h(q)=\iota_{n,n-1}(B_h(q)\otimes1).
\]
Choose an involution $d\in S_{2n-1}$ fixing one local point and pairing the
other $n-1$ local points with the complement.  The recovery map is
\[
 J\longmapsto\sum_{h,k}J(k,h)
       \widetilde B_h(q)^*B_d(q)\widetilde B_k(q).
\]
The endpoint minor uses the distinct rows indexed by $h^{-1}dk$.
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
The neighborhood on which the minor stays invertible may depend on $n$.
No sharp all-positive-parameter context bound is included.
\end{scope}
\provenance{D1215}{theory/sidequests/f1-limit/lifting.md}{theory/checks/f1\_limit\_check.py (L6); theory/checks/f1\_operational\_check.py (H11 at the endpoint)}{theory/verdicts/f1-limit-adjudication.md}

\begin{theorem}[Local contextual recovery of the Kraus Gram]
{\normalfont\statusProved}\quad
For every fixed $n\geq1$, some interval about one has the following property:
one retained context of total rank $2n-1$ recovers the full normalized-basis
Kraus Gram.  On that interval, equality in all retained contexts is
equivalent to pointwise Gram equality, and at each fibre to stable
scalar-unitary mixing.
\end{theorem}
\begin{scope}
The scalar unitary supplied by the finite Gram lemma is fibrewise.  A
continuous family of such unitaries is neither obtained nor required for
equality in the repaired core category.
\end{scope}
\provenance{F1-LIM-CTX}{theory/sidequests/f1-limit/lifting.md}{L6; H11 at q=1}{theory/verdicts/f1-limit-adjudication.md}
\begin{proof}
At one the intersection of the local subgroup with its $d$-conjugate is
trivial, so the $(n!)^2$ permutations $h^{-1}dk$ are distinct.  The selected
coefficient minor is therefore a permutation matrix.  Its entries vary
continuously, so its determinant remains nonzero near one.  The displayed
map is injective there.  Equality of its values forces equality of Grams;
conversely every retained channel is linear in the same Gram coefficients.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Operations on the full additive completion}

\begin{definition}[Controlled operations on completed objects]
Let $X=(r_n,p_{X,n})$ be a nonzero finite-support family of self-adjoint
matrix projections and put
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B_X&=\bigoplus_n p_{X,n}M_{r_n}(H_n^{\mathrm{cts}})p_{X,n},\\
 \theta_X(a)&=\sum_n(\operatorname{Tr}_{r_n}\otimes\tau_n)(a_n),\qquad
 w_X=\theta_X(1_X),\quad \tau_X=\theta_X/w_X.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Quantum atoms are such objects; separated words use
spatial tensor algebras and product traces, while classical wires retain the
uniform trace above.  Include every normalized preparation, discard and
finite POVM.  An instrument $X\to Y\underline O$ is a finite family
\[
 K_{o,i}\in\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y),\qquad
 \sum_{o,i}K_{o,i}^*K_{o,i}=1_X,
\]
realized by $(a_o)_o\mapsto\sum_{o,i}K_{o,i}^*a_oK_{o,i}$.  Retain
$K_{o,i}\otimes1_Z$, ordered parallel lists, traced assembly/split and finite
typed circuits.

The presentation uses the explicit sound classical wiring above.  At a fibre,
Kraus equality is coefficient-Gram equality, equivalently stable
scalar-unitary mixing within each outcome.  On an interval it is pointwise
coefficient-Gram equality, and for germs it is such equality on a common
smaller interval. Scalar-unitary cancellation proves congruence in each fibre; no continuous
choice of mixing unitary is required.  Any finite classical control is a
typed blockwise family of the admitted labels and contributes no additional
arbitrary CP map.  The resulting fibre, interval and germ categories retain
the realization and evaluation conventions above; their full circuit
realizations are not stipulated to be faithful.
\end{definition}
\begin{scope}
The construction assumes neither rigidity nor fusion and includes no
arbitrary CP-map data.  Fibrewise stable mixing does not assert the existence
of a continuous environmental unitary.
\end{scope}

\begin{theorem}[Operational completion of all finite projection objects]
{\normalfont\statusProved}\quad
With the displayed classical-wiring presentation and its UCP relations,
every nonzero object in the full finite completion carries normalized
states, effects, POVMs, retained instruments, traced assembly and finite
protocols.  These generate an ordered UCP circuit category with continuous
and germ evaluations.  Restriction to parabolic atoms recovers the preceding
operational formulas.
\end{theorem}
\begin{scope}
This statement uses the wiring and equality convention just stated.  It
asserts neither all-CP completeness, rigidity, fusion, faithful
circuit realization nor preservation of every additional endpoint equation.
\end{scope}

\begin{proof}
Preparations, discards, POVMs and Kraus lists give UCP maps exactly as in the
parabolic case.  The completed trace supplies the correct density dual, and
the unique traced expectations supply assembly and split.  Classical wiring
types every history relation and is parameter independent.  The free typed
category may therefore be quotiented by the proved congruence, and its
generator realization descends to a monoidal UCP functor.  The restriction
to rank-one parabolic projections identifies all formulas term by term.
\end{proof}
