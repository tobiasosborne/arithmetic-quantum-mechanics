## Local versus collective information

For a SINGLE orbit O the simultaneous fixed-vector space in ell^2(O)
is one-dimensional. Descending two such orbit factors independently
gives one fixed vector, whereas the joint same-orbit relation space above
has d dimensions. On the two-register version, the independently fixed
vector corresponds to the uniform vector over the relative index k.

The distinction is between separate invariant descent and collective
invariant descent after arithmetic assembly. Do not claim that the full
single-field observable algebra becomes scalar: orbit multiplicities and
other arithmetic operations can still carry information. The raw primitive
preparation vanishes at t=1; its graded conditional quantum remnant is the
candidate boundary. The construction must keep this scope explicit.

## Spectral questions after the finite result

On the full relational code, R has one d-cycle for each sigma-orbit O.
Consequently the proposed ordinary-trace identities are

    Tr(R^j)=q^gcd(n,j),
    det(I-zR)=product_(d|n)(1-z^d)^(c_d(q)/d).

The primitive common block has spectrum the n-th roots of unity,
independent of p. Distinguish the full multiplicity trace, the common-block
trace, and the reference expectation in the prepared pure logical state.

Uniform randomization over relative powers, with the classical random
outcome optionally discarded by a named channel, gives tracial states on
the common blocks. For a copied single POS reference followed by invariant
projection, the unnormalized reference weights on those blocks are
c_d(t^s)/d. Their moving-sector leading weights are proportional to
phi(d)/d. Record the randomization histories if retained; do not identify
different classical outcome sets along towers without an explicit map.

Study these finite identities before choosing an infinite-degree completion
or a regularization. An infinite unitary is not automatically trace class.
No Riemann spectral conclusion follows from the finite cycle calculation.
