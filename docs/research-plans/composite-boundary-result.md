relative Frobenius, 0 after identity, and 1/n after dephasing. All failure
histories retain their actual targets. Thus the first milestone has an
arithmetic quantum witness, not just a surviving dimension or formal label.

Fixed-base encoders identify the complete lower-period blocks in larger
extensions. These arithmetic corner maps and the derived first-grade
weights phi(d)/d yield a c0 completion of the moving matrix blocks.
Relative Frobenius is a unital CP automorphism of its minimal unitization,
outer there but spatially implemented on the named Hilbert sum. A positive
degree regulator supplies a faithful invariant state for every real beta>1,
with normalizer zeta(beta)/zeta(beta+1)-1 in the convergent real domain.

Finally, that completed state is the trace-norm limit of finite degree-tagged
arithmetic experiments performed before specialization, using an explicit
degree prior. Degree completion and t-to-one agree in both orders and
along every joined path, with a uniform integer-cutoff bound. The total
physical event remains rare, with the stated positive first-order rate.
The invariant randomized reference and the separate unrandomized quantum
Frobenius witness remain distinct preparations.

## Limits and remaining research

The source is a concrete arithmetic matrix/process construction on named
finite-field diagrams, not a unique universal quantization of all arithmetic
schemes. Its quantum boundary retains selected common observables and
specified processes; actual field multiplicities and additional physical
Fourier probabilities can still depend on p. No all-arithmetic comparison
or arbitrary future p-dependent operation is claimed.

The infinite completion, its unitization and the degree prior/regulator
are explicit choices whose arithmetic provenance has been demonstrated.
Their uniqueness has not been proved. The spectrum here is the unit circle
with root-of-unity eigenvalues. The zeta quotient comes from the derived
degree coefficients; it is not a construction of an operator with the
Riemann zeros as eigenvalues. The logarithmic degree regulator is central
and is not the nontrivial Frobenius dynamics.

Further exploration can test mixed Fourier/multiplication operations beyond
the admitted return, compare other completed representations and regulators,
and investigate what additional global arithmetic data could yield a
different spectral object. Those are subsequent research targets, rather
than hidden assumptions of the constructed quantum boundary.
