## Proposed parameters and comparison

Separate the incidence parameter q from the cyclotomic phase level N of the
existing D1001--D1007 framework. For an E-linear symplectic space E^(2n),
the arithmetic comparison point is q=|E|=p^r and N=p. The underlying quantum
system has rn prime-field sites. For prime-field isotropic contexts obtained
by restriction of scalars, the incidence parameter is instead p and the
symplectic rank is rn. These are two explicitly different context comparisons;
do not use the same symbol q to identify their ranks or flag sets.

The first proposal fixes a named finite field diagram at p, its integer
degrees, and its multiplication/embedding/trace tables; fixes N=p; and seeks
an incidence deformation q->1 with these phase data retained. It is not the
diagonal prescription N=q^(1/r)->1. A theory for varying N would require
additional compatible tables and is not asserted.

Choose, and record, an F_p-basis B_E of each field and its trace-dual basis.
Use a normal basis when a cyclic Frobenius matrix is useful; tower bases need
not be simultaneously normal and no such choice is assumed. The candidate
arithmetic comparison Q_(E,B) on the quantum registers sends |x> to its
coordinate tuple, W_E(a,b) to the tensor of prime-field Weyls with position
coordinates in B_E and momentum coordinates in its dual, and U_E to the
actual coordinate Frobenius permutation. It sends M_E to the reversible
controlled addition computed using the recorded multiplication tensor
mu_E, not to a gate chosen merely for its dimension or hierarchy label.

For every i:K->E, Q_(I,B) must send J_i and V_i to the matrices of the
actual included coordinate subspace and actual trace fibres. The code atom
must map to that included subspace; the logical momentum must still be
the trace quotient. These maps and their retained failure projectors are
the candidate comparison on the FRP source. Basis changes are named unitary
comparison maps, not evidence that the normal-basis labeling is canonical.

## Coupled context algebra to construct

At an arithmetic point retain the full context representation
K_E=C[X_C(E^(2n))] and the quantum register H_E^tensor n, together with:

- isotropic flags and their incident subspaces, including marked code
  isotropics N_i and their prime-field restrictions;
- the actual semilinear/Frobenius permutation on the context representation
  and U_E on the quantum representation;
- Weyl constraints controlled by the marked contexts, such as
  sum_F |F><F| tensor P_(L_F)^Z for a named isotropic phase subspace;
- arithmetic multiplication gates, code embeddings and trace-fibre
  reductions on the quantum factor, with the induced comparisons on any
  context labels for which such a comparison is actually defined;
- the existing type-C invariant incidence operators as a distinguished
  subalgebra, without passing the whole construction to their commutant.

For general isotropic Weyl constraints, commuting phase lifts and their
characters must be included, especially at p=2; the present N_i phase
constraints already have such a lift. There is no claim that a higher
multiplication gate maps every stabilizer context to a stabilizer context.
Its source and target remain the same quantum word, while any context update
must be separately defined or kept as a measurement record.

The proposed endpoint uses a signed coordinate apartment B_n for the
E-linear type-C context labels and the actual finite phase registers at
level N. One must construct the algebra generated by the endpoint incidence
operators, these *coupled* constraint operators, Frobenius and arithmetic
gates as limits of a common specified section algebra. The endpoint may be
noncommutative; no claim of a tensor-product algebra decomposition is made.
The missing work is the positive section algebra and a comparison functor on
the relation diagrams, not the ability to write down finite matrices at one.

## Reference trace and the Frobenius boundary

Name the reference functional on every proposed fibre. The phase component
uses normalized ordinary matrix trace at the fixed phase level N; the
incidence component is compared to the positive Hecke coefficient trace.
Whether these extend to a faithful positive trace on the coupled mixed-word
algebra is a theorem to prove, rather than a product-trace assumption.
The finite process densities remain ordinary trace-one matrices; switching
to coefficient-trace densities requires its explicit density-dual factors.

On an r-site normal-basis cyclic Frobenius, the fixed phase trace is
N^(1-r). For r>1 it stays below one when N>=2 and q tends to one. This
avoids imposing the *different* boundary prescription that substitutes p=1
into the physical arithmetic trace p^(1-r). The latter gives tau(U)=1;
if tau is faithful, positive and normalized by tau(1)=1, and U is unitary, then

    tau((U-1)^*(U-1))=2-tau(U)-tau(U^*)=0,

so U=1. This is a boundary condition on that named trace, not a universal
obstruction to other comparisons. The proposed independent-N rule must
still be derived from the stated coupled section algebra. Its existence
does not follow from avoiding this elementary obstruction.

## Six preregistered witness tests for the next stage

1. **Actual noncommutativity and Frobenius action.** For F4=F2[alpha],
   alpha^2=alpha+1, retain the source state |alpha> and the test projector
   |alpha><alpha|. The Born probability is one before U_E and zero after it.
   Also X(1) and Z(alpha) anticommute. Track these exact two arithmetic
   processes through the proposed Q_(I,B) and the endpoint comparison.
   A surviving abstract Frobenius symbol with identical observed channels
   fails this test. Check the chosen endpoint trace on U and (U-1)^*(U-1).
2. **Transfer and Fourier diagram.** At F2 subset F4 subset F16, compare
   the actual coordinate J,V and code projectors, then the diagrams
   V_j V_i=V_(ji), F_E J_i=V_i F_K and the two-tag Fourier decoder square.
   Check their images as maps on matrix units, with success probabilities
   and each failure branch. Individual endpoint gate lifts do not establish
   these diagram equations; a failure of any relation blocks the functor.
3. **Arithmetic multiplication retained.** Transport M_E^(2) and M_E^(3)
   using the actual multiplication tensor, compare their subfield logical
   actions, and mutate mu_E to addition or one incorrect product entry.
   A comparison that cannot detect that mutation has lost arithmetic gate
   data. Higher Clifford level labels alone do not meet this witness.
4. **Isotropic geometry rather than full commutants alone.** Enumerate the
   marked prime-field isotropic N_i, its quotient and the compatible E-linear
   isotropic flags. Retain the normalizer action and compare a controlled
   constraint before/after an actual Weyl or Frobenius process. A construction
   in which all these distinctions disappear after taking an invariant
   commutant fails the retained-structure target. Verify characteristic-two
   phase lifts explicitly, rather than importing an odd-prime relation theorem.
5. **Type-C independent composition.** Use the admitted D1251--D1254
   decomposable corner and shuffle maps Phi,Psi at arithmetic fibres. Check
   their proposed mixed-word extensions against independent quantum tensor
   and all four decoder outcomes, then compare both ternary shuffle routes.
   At q=1 use the B_m times B_n block inclusion and unsigned shuffle register.
   Since the block is nonparabolic, a generic Hecke tensor inclusion must
   not be inserted without a proof. Failure of exact channel/composition
   equality records the need for a correspondence, not a false tensor functor.
6. **Positivity and normalization across the interval.** For a proposed
   finite section basis, form the full mixed-word trace Gram matrix and the
   Choi matrices of every transfer and retained decoder, with the declared
   density convention. Test near one, at the arithmetic point, and between;
   mutate the fibre normalization or use the forbidden physical diagonal
   continuation. Any negative principal minor or failed completeness equation
   rejects the candidate. Finite green tests only guide the proof of positivity
   for every point of the proposed interval.
