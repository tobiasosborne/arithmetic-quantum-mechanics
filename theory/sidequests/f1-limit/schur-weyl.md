# Schur--Weyl quotients, assembly, and stable physical traces

Status: PROVED within the stated hypotheses. Admission and repaired scope
are recorded in `../../verdicts/f1-limit-adjudication.md`.
Definitions D1232--D1235 are recorded in `../../../definitions.md`.

Source `DM10` is Davydov--Molev, arXiv:1008.3739v2.  The introduction,
printed p. 3, states that the natural map
`C[S_n]->End_(gl_d)(V^tensor n)` is surjective and is injective for
`n<=d`.  Section 3.3, Proposition 3.4 and equation (3.4), printed p. 13,
states the full monoidal Schur--Weyl functor.  The trace, Gram expectation,
quantitative convergence, and finite-rank ideal bookkeeping below are local
derivations.

## 1. The commutant quotient and its kernel

<1>1. **ASSUME** `d>=1`, `n>=0`, `V=C^d`, and D1232.

<1>2. **PROVE** `rho_(d,n)` is an algebra star homomorphism into the
`U(d)` commutant.

<2>1. For elementary tensors, applying the definition twice gives
`P_sigma P_pi=P_(sigma pi)` and `P_1=1`.

<2>2. Permutation matrices are unitary, and
`P_sigma^*=P_(sigma^(-1))`; linear extension is a star homomorphism.

<2>3. The diagonal action is

    g(v_1 tensor ... tensor v_n)=gv_1 tensor ... tensor gv_n.

<2>4. Applying `P_sigma` before or after this action gives the same tensor,
so every `P_sigma` belongs to `End_(U(d))(V^tensor n)`. **QED**

<1>3. **PROVE** the target commutant equals the complex general-linear
commutant used in classical Schur--Weyl duality.

<2>1. An operator commuting with `U(d)` commutes infinitesimally with its
real Lie algebra `u(d)`.

<2>2. The complex span of `u(d)` is `gl_d(C)`, so it commutes with the
differentiated `gl_d(C)` action.

<2>3. Conversely, commuting with `gl_d(C)` implies commuting with the
exponentials of `u(d)`, hence with connected `U(d)`.

<2>4. Therefore
`End_(U(d))(V^tensor n)=End_(gl_d)(V^tensor n)`. **QED**

<1>4. **PROVE** `rho_(d,n)` is surjective.

<2>1. By <1>3 its target is the target of DM10 equation (3.4).

<2>2. Classical Schur--Weyl duality, stated in DM10 introduction p. 3 and
used in Proposition 3.4 p. 13, says that equation (3.4) is surjective.

<2>3. Its generators are the same position permutations as D1232, so the
homomorphism there is exactly `rho_(d,n)`. **QED**

<1>5. **PROVE** `rho_(d,n)` is faithful when `d>=n`.

<2>1. Choose distinct standard basis vectors `e_1,...,e_n` and put
`v=e_1 tensor ... tensor e_n`.

<2>2. The `n!` tensors `P_sigma v` have mutually distinct index words and
are therefore linearly independent.

<2>3. If `sum_sigma a_sigma P_sigma=0`, applying it to `v` forces every
`a_sigma=0`. **QED**

<1>6. **PROVE** `rho_(d,n)` is not faithful when `d<n`.

<2>1. Embed `S_(d+1)` on the first `d+1` positions and form its nonzero
antisymmetrizer

    A_(d+1)=sum_(sigma in S_(d+1)) sgn(sigma)sigma.

<2>2. Its action on the first `d+1` tensor factors has image
`wedge^(d+1)V=0`.

<2>3. Thus `A_(d+1)` is a nonzero element of the kernel. **QED**

<1>7. **PROVE** the exact finite-rank kernel description in D1232.

<2>1. Semisimplicity gives the Wedderburn decomposition

    C[S_n] ~= direct-sum_(lambda partition n) End(S^lambda),

where `S^lambda` is the Specht module.

<2>2. Classical Schur--Weyl decomposition refines <1>4 to

    V^tensor n ~= direct-sum_(lambda partition n, rows(lambda)<=d)
                     L_d(lambda) tensor S^lambda,

with each displayed `L_d(lambda)` nonzero.

<2>3. Hence the `lambda` Wedderburn block acts faithfully exactly when
`rows(lambda)<=d`; the direct sum of the remaining blocks is the kernel.

<2>4. The two-sided ideal generated by `A_(d+1)` has nonzero projection to
`End(S^lambda)` exactly when the restriction of `S^lambda` to
`S_(d+1)` contains its sign representation.

<2>5. The symmetric-group branching rule says this happens exactly when
the Young diagram of `lambda` has at least `d+1` rows: remove boxes down to
the first column of height `d+1`, and conversely such a sign constituent
forces that column.

<2>6. Thus the antisymmetrizer-generated ideal is precisely the sum of
blocks with more than `d` rows, which is the kernel from <2>3. **QED**

## 2. Coherent assembly

<1>8. **ASSUME** the standard block inclusion
`mu_(m,n):C[S_m] tensor C[S_n]->C[S_(m+n)]` from D1102 at `q=1`.

<1>9. **PROVE** the quotient maps form a homomorphism of multiplicative
sequences.

<2>1. Use the standard unitary associator
`V^tensor(m+n) ~= V^tensor m tensor V^tensor n`.

<2>2. For `sigma in S_m`, `pi in S_n`, direct application to an elementary
tensor gives

    rho_(d,m+n)(mu(sigma tensor pi))
      =rho_(d,m)(sigma) tensor rho_(d,n)(pi).

<2>3. Bilinearity proves the same equation for all algebra elements.

<2>4. It implies

    mu(ker rho_(d,m) tensor C[S_n]
       +C[S_m] tensor ker rho_(d,n))
       subseteq ker rho_(d,m+n).

<2>5. Therefore D1233's `nu_(m,n)` on quotient images is well-defined.

<2>6. Its associativity and unit diagrams are the ordinary tensor-product
associator and unit diagrams for the spaces `V^tensor n`; both composites
act identically on elementary tensors. **QED**

<1>10. **PROVE** the right-module Schur--Weyl functor is strong monoidal
and identify its structure map.

<2>1. For a right `C[S_n]`-module `M`, put

    F_d(M)=M tensor_(C[S_n]) V^tensor n,

where `V^tensor n` has the left permutation action and diagonal `U(d)`
action.  The two actions commute by <1>2.

<2>2. For `M` in degree `m` and `N` in degree `n`, D1222 gives

    F_d(M star N)
     =[(M tensor N) tensor_(C[S_m] tensor C[S_n]) C[S_(m+n)]]
          tensor_(C[S_(m+n)]) V^tensor(m+n).

<2>3. Cancel the consecutive extension and restriction of scalars by the
balanced map

    ((m tensor n) tensor g) tensor v
       |->(m tensor n) tensor rho_(d,m+n)(g)v.

<2>4. Using <2>2 of <1>9, the resulting balanced tensor product splits as

    (M tensor_(C[S_m]) V^tensor m)
       tensor (N tensor_(C[S_n]) V^tensor n).

<2>5. The composite is `U(d)`-equivariant for the diagonal tensor action.

<2>6. Balanced-tensor associativity makes the pentagon commute; degree zero
gives the unit.  This is the monoidal structure asserted by DM10
Proposition 3.4.

<2>7. This construction again uses **induction** in the source category.
The operator equality <1>9 should not be misread as an objectwise tensor of
an `S_m`-module and an `S_n`-module carrying an `S_(m+n)` action. **QED**

## 3. Normalized tensor trace

<1>11. **PROVE** for every `sigma in S_n`,

    tr_(d,n)(sigma)=d^(cyc(sigma)-n).

<2>1. In the tensor basis indexed by words
`i=(i_1,...,i_n) in {1,...,d}^n`, `P_sigma` fixes the basis vector exactly
when `i_j=i_(sigma^(-1)(j))` for every `j`.

<2>2. Such a word is constant on each cycle of `sigma`, and each of its
`cyc(sigma)` cycles may receive any of `d` labels.

<2>3. Hence `Tr(P_sigma)=d^cyc(sigma)`.  Divide by `dim V^tensor n=d^n`.
**QED**

<1>12. **PROVE** `tr_(d,n)->tau_n` coefficientwise at fixed `n`, with
the D1235 bound.

<2>1. The identity has `n` cycles, while every nonidentity permutation has
at most `n-1` cycles.

<2>2. Thus `tr_(d,n)(1)=1=tau_n(1)` and
`|tr_(d,n)(sigma)|<=d^(-1)` for `sigma!=1`.

<2>3. If `x=sum a_sigma sigma`, the triangle inequality gives

    |tr_(d,n)(x)-tau_n(x)|
       <=d^(-1)sum_(sigma!=1)|a_sigma|.

<2>4. In particular the convergence is uniform on coefficient-`l^1` bounded
subsets of each fixed group algebra. **QED**

<1>13. **PROVE** the trace radical at finite rank is exactly the
Schur--Weyl kernel.

<2>1. For any `x in C[S_n]`,

    tr_(d,n)(x^*x)=d^(-n)Tr(rho(x)^*rho(x)).

<2>2. The ordinary matrix trace is faithful, so this vanishes exactly when
`rho(x)=0`.

<2>3. Therefore `tr_(d,n)` descends to a faithful trace on the quotient
image, even when `d<n`, and its pullback radical is `ker rho_(d,n)`.

<2>4. The coefficient trace is faithful on the full group algebra, so when
`d<n` it cannot descend through the nonzero quotient ideal of <1>6. **QED**

## 4. Conditional expectations and their stable limit

<1>14. **ASSUME** `d>=n`, `K=S_m times S_(n-m)`, and `B=C[K]`.

<1>15. **PROVE** the physical trace-preserving conditional expectation has
the Gram formula D1234.

<2>1. By <1>5 and <1>13, `tr_(d,n)` is faithful on `C[S_n]`.

<2>2. The trace inner product is

    <k,h>_d=tr_(d,n)(k^*h)
           =d^(cyc(k^(-1)h)-n).

<2>3. The trace-preserving conditional expectation onto `B` is the
orthogonal projection for this inner product (finite Umegaki theorem, used
already in `F1-HCK-TOWER`).

<2>4. Writing `E_(d,K)(sigma)=sum_(h in K)c_hh`, orthogonality against
each `k in K` gives

    sum_(h in K)G_d(k,h)c_h
       =d^(cyc(k^(-1)sigma)-n).

<2>5. The restricted Gram matrix is positive definite by faithfulness, so
this system has a unique solution. **QED**

<1>16. **PROVE** `E_(d,K)` differs from coefficient deletion at finite
`d` in the smallest overlapping example.

<2>1. Take `n=3`, `K=<s_1>`, `sigma=s_2`, and `r=1/d`.

<2>2. In basis `(1,s_1)`, <1>15 gives

    G=[[1,r],[r,1]],             b=[r,r^2]^T.

<2>3. Since `G[r,0]^T=b`, uniqueness gives

    E_(d,K)(s_2)=d^(-1)1.

<2>4. Coefficient deletion gives `E_(infty,K)(s_2)=0`.  Thus neither the
finite trace nor its expectation is silently identified with the flag/group
coefficient structure. **QED**

<1>17. **PROVE** the quantitative expectation convergence in D1235.

<2>1. Retain `d>=n` from <1>14 and additionally assume `d>|K|-1`.
Put `N=|K|`. The Gram matrix has diagonal entries one and every
off-diagonal entry has modulus at most `d^(-1)` by <1>12.

<2>2. Thus `G=I+R` with coefficient row-sum norm
`||R||_infty<=(N-1)/d`.

<2>3. Since `d>N-1`, the Neumann series gives

    ||G^(-1)||_infty<=1/(1-(N-1)/d).

<2>4. If `sigma notin K`, then `k^(-1)sigma!=1` for every `k in K`, so
the right side vector in <1>15 has infinity norm at most `1/d`.

<2>5. Its solution consequently satisfies

    ||c||_infty<=1/(d-(N-1)).

<2>6. If `sigma in K`, a conditional expectation fixes it exactly.

<2>7. Linear extension proves coefficientwise
`E_(d,K)(x)->E_(infty,K)(x)` for every fixed `x`; for an off-subgroup
element the coefficient-`l^1` norm is at most
`N/(d-(N-1))`. **QED**

## 5. State and Born-probability comparison

<1>18. **PROVE** a fixed coefficient-state experiment has a controlled
large-`d` physical realization whenever its density remains nonzero in the
quotient.

<2>1. Let `h>=0`, `tau_n(h)=1`, and `0<=e<=1` in `C[S_n]`.

<2>2. Define for any `y=sum y_sigma sigma`

    Delta_d(y)=d^(-1)sum_(sigma!=1)|y_sigma|.

Then <1>12 gives `|tr_d(y)-tau_n(y)|<=Delta_d(y)`.

<2>3. If `Delta_d(h)<1`, then
`tr_d(h)>=1-Delta_d(h)>0`; hence
`rho_d(h)/tr_d(h)` is a physical normalized density on the quotient image.

<2>4. Its Born probability is `tr_d(he)/tr_d(h)`, while the coefficient
Born probability is `tau_n(he)`.

<2>5. Because `0<=tau_n(he)<=1`,

    |tr_d(he)/tr_d(h)-tau_n(he)|
      <=[Delta_d(he)+Delta_d(h)]/[1-Delta_d(h)].

<2>6. At fixed `h,e,n` the right side tends to zero.  This comparison is
about a fixed finite experiment as `d` grows; it asserts no uniformity when
`n`, the coefficient norms, or the experiment vary with `d`. **QED**

<1>19. **CONCLUDE** `F1-LIM-SW`: the symmetric endpoint has coherent
Schur--Weyl quotient fibres, faithful in degree `n` exactly from `d=n`
onward, with the explicit finite-rank antisymmetrizer kernel.

<1>20. **CONCLUDE** `F1-LIM-SWTRACE`: normalized tensor trace converges to
the coefficient trace at each fixed degree, physical conditional
expectations converge through their invertible Gram systems, and the
displayed state bound quantifies the operational comparison. **QED**
