## 1. Why the graded amplitude category is not yet rigid

<1>1. **ASSUME** the universal positive Hecke composition category `U_q`
of D1224 and its generator `x` in degree one.

<1>2. **PROVE** `x` cannot have a right or left dual inside `U_q`.

<2>1. Every object of `U_q` is a finite direct sum of retracts of objects
having nonnegative integer degree.

<2>2. Tensor adds degrees and Homs between unequal degrees are zero by
D1221--D1224.

<2>3. If any object `y=direct-sum_(k>=0)y_k` were a right dual of `x`, its
evaluation would have type

    ev:x tensor y -> 1.

<2>4. Every summand `x tensor y_k` has degree `1+k>0`, including when
`k=0`. The unit has degree zero, so this entire Hom space is zero. This
covers mixed-degree finite direct sums and their retracts as well.

<2>5. A zero evaluation cannot satisfy the zigzag

    x -> x tensor y tensor x -> x =1_x

because `x` is nonzero.

<2>6. The same argument with the other zigzag excludes a left dual.
**QED**

<1>3. **PROVE** D1236 states the exact positive replacement.

<2>1. Supply the named strong monoidal comparison `i:U_q->R` of D1236.
Write `y=i(x)`. It is not assumed faithful: an algebra quotient may be part
of the comparison. In the positive version it is star-preserving. Generate
the additive idempotent-complete target by oriented tensor words in
`y` and the named `y^vee`.

<2>2. Supply the four typed structural maps (right and left versions)

    coev_R:1->y tensor y^vee,  ev_R:y^vee tensor y->1,
    coev_L:1->y^vee tensor y,  ev_L:y tensor y^vee->1.

<2>3. Impose the two right and two left zigzag equations with the chosen
associators.  These equations, rather than an algebra trace alone, make the
extension rigid.

<2>4. A pivotal structure identifies the two iterated-dual functors; a
spherical condition makes left and right traces equal.

<2>5. For a dagger realization, require evaluation and coevaluation to be
adjoint up to the stated pivotal normalization, and require the resulting
categorical dimensions to be positive.

<2>6. With this data, `(a tensor b)^vee=b^vee tensor a^vee`; nested cups
and caps give its zigzags by the generator zigzags. The dual of a finite sum
is the sum of duals, with block evaluation. For an idempotent `p`, its dual
idempotent satisfies `(p^vee)^2=p^vee` by contravariance; split it in the
target's Karoubi closure and restrict the cup and cap. The restricted
zigzags are the identities of the two retracts. Thus all generated sums
and retracts have duals, not only tensor words.

<2>7. These are sufficient typed comparison data; any rigid comparison
of this form supplies them by restriction. Fully faithful extension is a
separate property of `i`, never inferred from the cups and caps. **QED**

## 2. A concrete rigid target at the symmetric endpoint

<1>4. **ASSUME** `q=1`, choose `d>=1`, and let `V=C^d` with its defining
unitary `U(d)` action.

<1>5. **PROVE** Schur--Weyl supplies a positive rigid extension after the
dual choice is added.

<2>1. Let `R_d` be the full replete C*-tensor subcategory of finite
dimensional unitary `U(d)`-representations generated by `V`, its conjugate
dual `V^*`, finite sums and subobjects.

<2>2. The ordinary pairing and copairing are

    ev:V^* tensor V->C,       phi tensor v |->phi(v),
    coev:C->V tensor V^*,     1 |->sum_i e_i tensor e_i^*.

<2>3. Direct basis contraction proves both zigzag identities.

<2>4. The standard Hilbert dagger and the usual pivotal identification
`V~=V**` give positive dimension `d` and the ordinary matrix trace.

<2>5. Define `i_d:U_1->R_d` by `x|->V`, by `rho_(d,n)` on each tensor
power, and by the ranges of the image projections on sums and retracts.
schur-weyl.md proves tensor coherence; star is preserved by the unitary
permutation representation. Its image is the Schur--Weyl quotient image,
and `i_d` is not asserted faithful in degrees `n>d`.

<2>6. The target's additive Karoubi closure also contains the mixed words
in `V,V^*`, with the displayed cups and caps. This defines the rigid closure
of the quotient image, rather than a fully faithful embedding of `U_1`.

<2>7. This is a genuine positive rigid target.  It has infinitely many
simple objects as degrees vary, so it does not by itself meet the finiteness
condition in D1237. **QED**

## 3. What a fusion quotient must contain

<1>6. **PROVE** the package D1237 is the minimum typed quotient data needed
for a fusion comparison.

<2>1. A family of algebra ideals `I_n triangleleft End(x^tensor n)` gives
degreewise quotients but need not respect tensor.

<2>2. Requiring

    f in I_m  => f tensor g in I_(m+n),
    g in I_n  => f tensor g in I_(m+n)

for all `f,g`, and the analogous closure under arbitrary pre- and
postcomposition, makes `I` a monoidal categorical ideal.

<2>3. Requiring `I^*=I` makes the quotient dagger well-defined.

<2>4. Duality descends only if the ideal is closed under bending morphisms
with the chosen evaluations and coevaluations.

<2>5. At a nonsemisimple specialization, quotienting by the negligible
ideal

    Neg={f:Tr(gf)=0 for every composable g}

requires a chosen pivotal trace.  Without that trace, `Neg` is undefined.

<2>6. Additive and Karoubi completion then splits the surviving finite
direct sums and projections.

<2>7. The result is fusion only after checking: semisimplicity, simple
unit, finite-dimensional Homs, duals, and finitely many simple isomorphism
classes.  These conditions do not follow merely from finite-dimensionality
of each individual Hecke algebra.

<2>8. Thus the quotient ideal, dagger, trace/duality compatibility and
finiteness check in D1237 are each independently necessary, and together
are sufficient by the definition of a unitary fusion category. **QED**

## 4. The Temperley--Lieb quotient and its trace

<1>7. **ASSUME** `q>0` and put

    f_i=(q-T_i)/(q+1),
    delta=(q+1)/sqrt(q)=sqrt(q)+1/sqrt(q).

<1>8. **PROVE** `f_i` is a projection and identify the rank-two quotient
normalization.

<2>1. Since `T_i` is self-adjoint with eigenvalues `q,-1`, `f_i` is the
spectral projection for eigenvalue `-1`.

<2>2. Hence `f_i=f_i^*=f_i^2`.

<2>3. GW93 section 0.2, pp. 310--312, gives the type-A Temperley--Lieb
quotient relation in this Iwahori normalization:

    f_i f_(i+1) f_i=[q/(q+1)^2]f_i=delta^(-2)f_i.

The reflected relation with `i` and `i+1` exchanged holds as well.

<2>4. If `U_i=delta f_i`, then

    U_i^2=delta U_i,       U_iU_(i+1)U_i=U_i,

which is the loop-normalized Temperley--Lieb presentation.

<2>5. GW93 pp. 313--315 identifies the surviving generic Hecke simples as
those with at most two rows.  Thus this is a specified rank-two tensor
quotient, not a property of the full flag category. **QED**

<1>9. **PROVE** the coefficient/flag trace cannot be the Jones trace on
this quotient.

<2>1. Directly from D1101,

    tau_n(f_i)=q/(q+1),

because `tau_n(T_i)=0` and `tau_n(1)=1`.

<2>2. The normalized Markov/Jones trace compatible with the diagrammatic
cap has

    tr_J(f_i)=delta^(-2)=q/(q+1)^2;

ILZ17 equation (2.2), pp. 2--3, records the recursion after the explicit
translation `q_ILZ=-sqrt(q)`, `U_i=delta f_i`: its loop convention is
`U_i^2=-(q_ILZ+q_ILZ^(-1))U_i=delta U_i`.

<2>3. These values already differ by the factor `q+1` at level two.

<2>4. More categorically, the Temperley--Lieb map has a nonzero kernel from
level three on, while the coefficient trace is faithful by `F1-HCK-POS`.

<2>5. If it factored as `tau=bar(tau) after pi`, a nonzero `z` in the
kernel would satisfy
`0<tau(z^*z)=bar(tau)(pi(z^*z))=0`, a contradiction.

<2>6. Thus a Jones/Markov trace is mandatory extra state data and cannot be
obtained by descending the flag trace. **QED**

<1>10. **PROVE** the discrepancy at `q=1` agrees with the physical
two-dimensional Schur--Weyl quotient.

<2>1. At `q=1`, let `s=(12)` and

    e_+=(1+s)/2,             e_-=(1-s)/2=f_1.

<2>2. The coefficient trace gives
`tau_2(e_+)=tau_2(e_-)=1/2`.

<2>3. On `(C^2)^tensor2`, `e_+` projects onto the three-dimensional
symmetric subspace and `e_-` onto the one-dimensional alternating subspace.

<2>4. Normalized physical matrix trace therefore gives weights

    (tr_(2,2)(e_+),tr_(2,2)(e_-))=(3/4,1/4).

<2>5. The last value is `delta^(-2)=1/4` at `delta=2`, as required by the
Jones trace.

<2>6. BFK00 identifies this `delta=2` tower with the `sl_2` tensor-power
centralizers (introduction pp. 1--2; section 2.2 pp. 6--8).  Adding its
standard cups and caps gives the positive rigid spin-one-half category.

<2>7. Its Clebsch--Gordan labels are unbounded, so this positive endpoint is
rigid but not a finite fusion category. **QED**

## 5. Root-of-unity semisimplification and Fibonacci

<1>11. **PROVE** the unitary Fibonacci branch requires a different
parameter and a trace-radical quotient.

<2>1. On the positive real branch,

    delta=sqrt(q)+1/sqrt(q)>=2

by the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality, with equality only at `q=1`.

<2>2. The Fibonacci loop value is
`phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2=2cos(pi/5)<2`.

<2>3. Therefore no positive real `q`, and in particular not `q=1`, gives
the Fibonacci loop value through D1238.

<2>4. KL08 section 3, Theorem 2 and its corollary, PDF pp. 10--12, derives
`delta=phi` (up to sign) and a unitary Jones braid choice.

<2>5. In the present Hecke convention choose

    q_H=exp(2 pi i/5),       v=sqrt(q_H)=exp(pi i/5),
    q_ILZ=-v,               U_i^(ILZ)=delta f_i.

Then `-(q_ILZ+q_ILZ^(-1))=v+v^(-1)=2cos(pi/5)=phi`, and
`q_ILZ^2=q_H` has order five. The ILZ symbol for its loop generator is
therefore distinguished from our idempotent `f_i` by the factor `delta`.

<2>6. Raw root-of-unity Temperley--Lieb algebras need not be semisimple.
ILZ17 constructs the Jones quotient by the trace radical and its truncated
fusion product (Propositions 8.1 and 8.4, pp. 18--20).

<2>7. At order five, the surviving even labels are `{0,2}` and ILZ17
Proposition 8.4 gives

    2 tensor 2=0 direct-sum 2.

<2>8. Proposition 8.6, pp. 20--21, identifies the Jones quotients as
endomorphism algebras in the reduced quantum-`sl_2` fusion category.

<2>9. Thus the Fibonacci result uses, in order: the TL tensor ideal,
diagrammatic duals, a root-of-unity star/gauge supporting unitarity, the
Jones trace, its radical quotient, Karoubi completion, and restriction to
the even subcategory.  Each datum is visible in D1237--D1239. **QED**

## 6. Comparison map and scope

<1>12. **PROVE** the three branches fit into explicit maps rather than an
analogy.

<2>1. The universal composition branch is

    C[H_*] -> Add/Kar(C[H_*]) ~= finite right H_*-modules,

with induction tensor by `universal.md`.

<2>2. At `q=1`, Schur--Weyl gives the monoidal quotient maps

    C[S_n] -> End_(U(d))((C^d)^tensor n)

and the rigid positive enlargement is the category generated by `V,V^*`
with the maps in <1>5.

<2>3. The TL branch gives monoidal quotient maps

    H_n(q) -> TL_n(delta)

only after imposing <1>8's ideal; the diagram category supplies the rigid
maps and its Markov trace supplies physical normalization.

<2>4. The root-of-unity branch further maps `TL_n(delta)` to its Jones
quotient by the named trace radical and then to the even fusion subcategory.

<2>5. The coefficient/flag trace belongs to the first branch.  The ordinary
tensor trace belongs to the finite-dimensional Schur--Weyl branch.  The
Jones trace belongs to the TL/fusion branch.  Sections 4--5 compute why they
cannot be silently identified.

<2>6. These maps give viable rigid and fusion targets while preserving the
exact additional choices on which each target depends. **QED**

<1>13. **CONCLUDE** `F1-LIM-RIGID`: `U_q` is not itself rigid, but D1236
gives the required extension datum and Schur--Weyl at `q=1` supplies a
concrete positive realization.

<1>14. **CONCLUDE** `F1-LIM-TL`: TL and Fibonacci are explicit quotient
branches with independent duality and trace data; the faithful flag trace
does not descend, and Fibonacci is not the positive `q=1` fibre. **QED**
